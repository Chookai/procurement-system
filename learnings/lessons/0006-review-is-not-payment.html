Review is not payment.

Keep supplier claims, accepted deliveries and review decisions as separate facts.

Your win: explain why reviewing a MYR 1,200 invoice leaves four units outstanding.

Delivery facts

10 ordered · 6 accepted

4 outstanding

Owned by the issued PO and receipt history.

Invoice review

6 billed × MYR 200

MYR 1,200 · Reviewed by Eva

A reasoned review event; no payment action.

In our application, the invoice represents what the supplier billed. The review represents Eva’s examination. Neither changes how many goods Dinesh accepted. These project rules are captured in the invoice walkthrough.

Read the states independently

Invoice arrives before full delivery

Eva captures billed facts and links issued PO terms and optional receipt evidence. Outstanding remains four. Difference warnings help her investigate.

Something needs explanation

Eva records Needs clarification with a reason. The original invoice stays unchanged. A follow-up review records later findings; earlier reasons remain visible.

Eva records Reviewed

The review freezes her reason and the checks she saw. Four units still remain outstanding. Reviewed does not mean paid, formally reconciled or free of every discrepancy.

Follow the application boundary

Invoices.tsx → main.tsx invoiceCommand()
  capture billed lines or record a review decision
app.py + contracts.py
  validate identity, CSRF and command fields
workflow.py
  Eva only; current version; lock parent PR
invoicing.py
  capture immutable invoice + issued evidence
  OR append review with reason + checks snapshot
amounts.py
  calculate exact decimal line totals
PostgreSQL transaction
  invoice/review + PR version + audit + retry response
  commit together or roll back together

A database unique constraint protects the normalized supplier + invoice-number pair. This is different from an idempotency key: the key identifies a command retry; the supplier invoice number identifies an already-captured business document. See PostgreSQL 17: Unique constraints.

Azure-specific

In the proposed Azure deployment, Azure Database for PostgreSQL would host the same invoice, review and uniqueness rules. Those constraints do not become financial authorization just because the database is managed. The application still owns who may review, what the review means and which later capabilities can initiate payment.

Current implementation runs locally. Binary evidence ingestion, Azure hosting and the real RAG journey are separate remaining work; Foundry remains deferred.
